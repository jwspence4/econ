\section*{Questions for Review}

\begin{enumerate}

\item Money is a medium of exchange and a unit of account.

\item Commodity money has intrinsic value. Fiat money does not. Fiat money only has value because we've decreed it has value. We use fiat money

\item Demand deposits are deposits that you can withdraw whenever you want. Typically, they're checking account deposits. They're included in the money stock because you can use a debit card to pay with deposits just as easily as you can use cash.

\item The Fed sets monetary policy. The members of the Fed who vote on monetary policy are split between Presidential appointees and regionally appointed officials.

\item It buys government bonds.

\item If they lend out some of their deposits, they can charge interest on it, and earn a profit. Reserves and the money supply are related as follows:
	\begin{gather*}
	S_M = C + D \\
	D = \text{res} \cdot MM = \text{res} \cdot \frac{1}{\text{res ratio}} \\
	S_M = C + \text{res} \cdot \frac{1}{\text{res ratio}} 
	\end{gather*}

\end{enumerate}

A credit card payment is a store of value -- although not a reliable one -- because it is a promise to pay later. It’s not a unit of account because prices are denoted in today's dollars, not future dollar payments.

	\begin{enumerate}
	
	\item Increase -- the Fed buys bonds with newly created dollars, so it adds more money to the money supply.
	
	\item Increase -- a lower reserve requirement lowers the reserve ratio. That increases the money multiplier which in turn increases money creation by banks.
	
	\item Decrease -- a higher interest rate on reserves incentivizes banks to hold more reserves. More reserves leads to a higher reserve ratio and therefore a lower money multiplier. A lower money multiplier decreases money creation by banks.
	
	\item Decrease -- the Fed takes the money it receives out of circulation, so the money supply decreases.
	
	\item Increase -- when people deposit their currency at banks, banks then make loans using the deposits. The process of taking deposits and making loans generates money, so the money supply increases.
	
	\item Decrease -- an increase in reserves leads to an increase in the reserve ratio. An increase in the reserve ratio decreases the money multiplier. When the money multiplier decreases, banks create less money, so the money supply shrinks. 
	
	\item Decrease -- the Fed increases its target for the federal funds rate by increasing the interest it pays on reserves. When the Fed increases the interest it pays on reserves, the money supply decreases for the reasons in part (c). 
	
	\end{enumerate}

Overall, if Daddy Warbucks keeps his \$10 million in cash, then reserves will decrease by \$10 million in the banking system as a whole. \item  If the government buys \$10 million of government bonds and people hold all \$10 million in currency, then the money supply will increase by \$10 million dollars. That's the smallest possible increase in the money supply because banks can't generate any additional money if people don't make any additional deposits. 
	
	If people deposit the original \$10 million, and if people continually deposit the money that banks loan out from that original \$10 million, then reserves will increase by \$10 million. If banks don't hold excess reserves, then the reserve ratio will be 10\%, so the money multiplier will be $1/0.1 = 10$. That means deposits will increase by $10 \cdot \$10 \text{million} = \$100 \text{ million}$. That's the largest possible increase in the money supply because that's the most additional money that banks can generate.
	
\item The Fed buying \$2,000 worth of bonds will increase the money supply more. If people don't deposit the new currency, then the money supply will increase by \$2,000. If people do deposit the new currency and if people continually deposit the money that banks loan out from those new deposits, reserves will increase by \$2,000. If reserves increase by \$2,000, then deposits will increase by $\$2,000 \cdot \frac{1}{0.05} = \$40,000$, so the money supply will increase by \$40,000.
	
	If someone deposits \$2,000 worth of currency and people don't deposit the money that banks loan out from those new deposits, the money supply doesn't change. Deposits increase by \$2,000, but currency decreases by \$2,000. If people continually deposit the money that banks loan out from those new deposits, reserves increase by \$2,000, and deposits increase by \$40,000 for the same reason as above. That said, currency still decreases by \$2,000, so the money supply only increases by $- \$2,000 + \$40,000 = \$38,000.$

\end{enumerate}
